# Existing maps, sites and apps for Eucharistic miracles and Catholic sacred sites (state as of 13 Nov 2026)

Scope note: KronAtlas and Summit are excluded by instruction. All checks were made on 13 Nov 2026 by web search and page fetch. "Fetched" means I loaded the page itself; "search summary" means the fact comes from a search-result summary of that page and was not independently opened.

## 1. What is the current state of Carlo Acutis's own exhibition and catalogue online? Does it have a map? What are its terms of use?

### Takeaway
The official catalogue (miracolieucaristici.org) is still online and unchanged in form: a plain list of links to PDF panels grouped by country, in about 17 languages, with no interactive map, no search, no timeline, and no visible licence or terms of use. Panels are offered as free downloads for exhibiting; nothing grants a right to republish the text or images, and one independent developer who rebuilt the site treats the content as copyrighted.

### Cited Findings
- The list page credits the exhibition to Carlo Acutis and Nicola Gori and organises it by country headings with entries in the form "City, year". Counting the links: 108 entries under 20 countries (Italy 32, Spain 19, France 12, Germany 9, Holland 8, Belgium 7, Poland 5, most others 1-3), plus thematic sections: 2 intro panels, 20 "Saints, Mystics and the Eucharist", 5 "Our Lady and the Eucharist", 18 "Miraculous Communions" (about 151 entries overall). The page itself states no total. (Fetched) — [miracolieucaristici.org list](https://www.miracolieucaristici.org/en/liste/list.html)
- The same page contains no map, no copyright or terms-of-use text, and no mention of an app. It says exhibiting requires a "special high definition PDF" set of 166 posters at 60x80 cm. Contact: Associazione Amici di Carlo Acutis, info@carloacutis.com and info@miracolieucaristici.org. (Fetched) — [miracolieucaristici.org list](https://www.miracolieucaristici.org/en/liste/list.html)
- The home page is only a language bar: Italian, English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Russian, Greek, Hungarian, Dutch, Polish, Vietnamese, Filipino, Chinese, Japanese, Arabic, Urdu, a Sri Lanka PDF, and "Other languages" (a download page). No map, no terms, no date signals, no operator statement. (Fetched) — [miracolieucaristici.org](https://www.miracolieucaristici.org/)
- The download section offers print panels (high resolution 60x80 cm), "Web panels" "for all other uses", and editable brochure/poster PDFs. (Search summary) — [miracolieucaristici.org download](https://www.miracolieucaristici.org/download.html)
- The association's own page says that in Italy hosts must email info@carloacutis.com or phone; for all other countries "you can download the Exhibition free of charge in various languages" from the Download section. It gives no copyright notice and mentions no official app or map. It says the exhibition is on show on all five continents, and in the USA alone in thousands of parishes and 100 university campuses. (Fetched) — [carloacutis.com association page](https://carloacutis.com/en/association/mostra-miracoli-eucaristici)
- The association also lists three sister exhibitions by Carlo: Marian apparitions and shrines ("The Appeals of Our Lady"), "Angels and Demons", and "Inferno, Purgatory and Paradise". (Fetched) — [carloacutis.com association page](https://carloacutis.com/en/association/mostra-miracoli-eucaristici)
- Diocesan flyers credit the Associazione Amici di Carlo Acutis and say material is used with permission; one handout says that once permission is given you download and print the panels you want. (Search summary) — [Diocese of Charleston flyer](https://charlestondiocese.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Eucharistic-Miracles-Flyer-EN.pdf); [exhibition handout](https://d5qysv7pc67gd.cloudfront.net/pdf/Eucharistic-Exhibition-Handout.pdf)
- A developer who rebuilt the catalogue in 2026 states he does not own the data, calls it copyrighted, and keeps it behind an authenticated Cloudflare Worker rather than publishing it. (Fetched) — [Tommy Smale write-up](https://tomportfolio.netlify.app/posts/2026-05-27-eucharistic-miracles/); [GitHub README](https://github.com/thsmale/eucharistic-miracles-ui)
- Conflict on "does the original have a map": a Holy Heroes blog post and a search summary of the NC Register say Carlo's site includes a world map showing each miracle; the pages I fetched (home and English list) have none. — [Holy Heroes blog](https://blog.holyheroes.com/have-you-heard-of-blessed-carlos-website-of-eucharistic-miracles/); contradicted by [miracolieucaristici.org list](https://www.miracolieucaristici.org/en/liste/list.html)
- The US mirror, The Real Presence Eucharistic Education and Adoration Association, lists miracles by country with a separate static "Map: Eucharistic Miracles of [country]" panel per country alongside the per-miracle PDFs (search summary). Its HTTPS certificate was expired when I tried to fetch it on 13 Nov 2026, so I could not read its permission text. — [therealpresence.org](https://www.therealpresence.org/eucharst/mir/engl_mir.htm)
- Counts quoted for the catalogue vary by source: 163 stories (Holy Heroes), "over 150" (search summary of an unofficial site), about 151 by my link count, 166 posters on the official list page. — [Holy Heroes blog](https://blog.holyheroes.com/have-you-heard-of-blessed-carlos-website-of-eucharistic-miracles/); [miracolieucaristici.org list](https://www.miracolieucaristici.org/en/liste/list.html)

### Inferences
- The official site is a download point for a printed exhibition, not a web product. Its only "maps" are static per-country panel images. The world-map claims in secondary sources most likely refer to those panels or to an older version of the site.
- The 108 place-based miracles in 20 countries used by Carlo's Compass matches the country-grouped part of the official list exactly; the other ~43 entries are saints, Marian and communion panels that are not place-based miracles in the same sense.
- Absence of a licence means default copyright applies to the panel text and photographs (author credit: Carlo Acutis and Nicola Gori; rights apparently exercised by the Associazione Amici di Carlo Acutis). Facts (place, year, what happened, who approved) are not protected, so writing original 2-3 sentence summaries with links back to the official PDFs is the low-risk route; copying panel text or photos would need the association's permission.
- The catalogue's per-miracle panels do not, as far as the list page shows, carry a structured approval status or source list; that is a gap a cited card can fill.

### Gaps
- I could not read the wording of any permission statement on therealpresence.org (expired certificate) or the full text of the official download page, so the exact conditions for non-exhibition reuse are unconfirmed. Only an email to info@miracolieucaristici.org would settle it.
- Whether the association has updated the catalogue since the September 2025 canonization is unknown; the pages carry no dates.
- Whether an older version of the official site had an interactive world map was not checked (would need the Wayback Machine).

## 2. Which interactive maps of Eucharistic miracles exist?

### Takeaway
Only two real interactive Eucharistic-miracle maps were found, both launched in 2026: the Malvern shrine's free mobile app (made with Carlo's mother, 13,000+ downloads) and one developer's web remake, eucharistic-miracles.net. Neither has a timeline, certainty-of-location marking, or per-miracle Church-approval status; no ArcGIS StoryMap, Google My Maps, or National Eucharistic Revival map surfaced.

### Cited Findings

**A. Saint Carlo Acutis: Eucharistic Miracles of the World (App Store name "St Carlo: Eucharistic Miracles")**
- Maker: St. Carlo Acutis Shrine and Center for Eucharistic Encounter at Malvern Retreat House, Malvern, Pennsylvania; led by executive director Mary Bea Damico; developed in collaboration with Antonia Acutis (Carlo's mother). Launched 14 Feb 2026. — [Vatican News](https://www.vaticannews.va/en/church/news/2026-02/st-carlo-acutis-eucharistic-miracles-new-mobile-app.print.html)
- App Store listing: seller Voveo Marketing Group, copyright Malvern Retreat House, free, no in-app purchases listed, 157 MB, Education category, 5.0 stars from 24 ratings, language listed as English only. Version 1.0 on 12 Feb, 1.0.1 on 18 Feb (fixed "an external maps bug"), 1.0.2 on 24 Feb, 1.0.3 on 6 Aug (reminder fix). (Fetched) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/6758726547)
- Also on Google Play. (Search summary) — [Zeale News](https://zeale.co/news/articles/new-app-inspired-by-st-carlo-acutis-encourages-learning-about-eucharistic-miracles)
- Features per NC Register: miracle stories taken from Carlo's website; an interactive map showing miracle locations by country; search by category (examples given: "doubting priest", "natural disaster"); links to pilgrimage-planning resources per site; links to Old and New Testament verses per miracle; "Live Like Carlo" timeline of his life (a biography timeline, not a miracle timeline); online adoration streams; daily Carlo quote and "Miracle of the Day". — [NC Register](https://www.ncregister.com/features/how-st-carlo-acutis-inspired-this-eucharistic-miracles-app)
- Reach: more than 13,000 downloads in 132 countries; "translated into six languages". — [NC Register](https://www.ncregister.com/features/how-st-carlo-acutis-inspired-this-eucharistic-miracles-app); the six-language claim is contradicted by the [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/6758726547), which lists English only
- Miracle count: no source states a total. A volunteer says the shrine's collection started with five miracles and 16 more were added; an App Store reviewer says "100+". — [NC Register](https://www.ncregister.com/features/how-st-carlo-acutis-inspired-this-eucharistic-miracles-app); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/6758726547)
- Origin: Damico conceived the map idea in April 2025 in Rome and spoke with Antonia Acutis in July 2025. No article describes a formal licence from the association. — [NC Register](https://www.ncregister.com/features/how-st-carlo-acutis-inspired-this-eucharistic-miracles-app)
- Further coverage in March 2026. — [Aleteia](https://aleteia.org/2026/03/12/st-carlos-life-and-miracles-theres-an-app-for-that/)
- Maintained: yes, last update 6 Aug (2026 by inference from the February 2026 launch). — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/6758726547)

**B. eucharistic-miracles.net (web)**
- Maker: Tommy Smale, individual developer; write-up dated 1 June 2026. Described as a modern, mobile-friendly, community-driven remake of Carlo's site. — [write-up](https://tomportfolio.netlify.app/posts/2026-05-27-eucharistic-miracles/)
- Features: four views (data table default on desktop, map, cards, accordion default on mobile); search by country or city; filters for country, category and similar; sorting; per-miracle pages with image carousel, social sharing and a link to the original PDF. No timeline mentioned. — [write-up](https://tomportfolio.netlify.app/posts/2026-05-27-eucharistic-miracles/)
- Tech: React, Redux, React Router, react-simple-maps (SVG from GeoJSON, chosen to avoid third-party tile calls), Cloudflare Pages plus a Worker. — [write-up](https://tomportfolio.netlify.app/posts/2026-05-27-eucharistic-miracles/)
- Open source front end at github.com/thsmale/eucharistic-miracles-ui: 88 commits, 1 star, 0 forks, no licence file, and the miracle data is not in the repo ("behind auth" because the author does not own it). — [GitHub](https://github.com/thsmale/eucharistic-miracles-ui)
- Free, no business model stated. The live site is a JavaScript app; a plain fetch returned only the title, so I could not verify its current content or miracle count. — [eucharistic-miracles.net](https://eucharistic-miracles.net/)

**C. SkySurfer "Map of Eucharistic Miracles" (web, unfinished)**
- A zoomable photo mosaic (credited to Mosaically) with a country search, not a true map. Markers are meant to link out to Carlo's site, but the page says "TO BE CONTINUED WITH ADDING MARKERS BASED ON APPROVAL". Footer: Copyright 2026 SkySurfer, all rights reserved. (Fetched) — [sky-surfer.com](https://www.sky-surfer.com/eucharisticmiraclesbystcarlosacutis)

**D. Stacker data story (static charts, 2023)**
- Published 13 June 2023 by Lauren Liebhaber with charts by Emma Rubin, using the miracolieucaristici.org list (excluding relics and feasts): one regional distribution map and one chart over time, peaking in the Late Middle Ages. Says almost half of the miracles involve hosts or wine taking on traits of flesh and blood, and nearly two dozen involve hosts surviving flood, fire or theft. Syndicated widely to local news sites. Page shows "all rights reserved"; no data download. — [Stacker](https://stacker.com/stories/news/vatican-investigating-miracle-connecticut-church-heres-every-eucharistic-miracle)

**E. Real Presence Association country map panels (static)**
- Per-country static map panels beside the PDFs; not interactive. (Search summary; site certificate expired on 13 Nov 2026.) — [therealpresence.org](https://www.therealpresence.org/eucharst/mir/engl_mir.htm)

**F. Unofficial "carloacutis.it.com"**
- Appeared in search results with a miracles section, study guide and exhibition guide, but the domain did not resolve on 13 Nov 2026. — [carloacutis.it.com/miracles](https://carloacutis.it.com/miracles)

### Inferences
- The direct competitor is the Malvern app. Its advantages are the family's involvement, Vatican News coverage, and an installed base. Its limits, as far as sources show: mobile only (no web map), map organised by country, no miracle timeline, no stated approval status or source list per miracle, no location-certainty marking, closed content.
- eucharistic-miracles.net is the closest web product in concept (searchable, filterable, map view, link to original PDF) but is table-first, has a simple SVG map without era filtering, and has no reusable data.
- Nobody found combines a historical map, a draggable timeline, and cited cards with recognition and location-certainty badges. That combination is open ground.
- The canonization produced a small wave of hobby projects in 2026; two of the four non-official efforts found are already unfinished or offline, which suggests maintenance is the common failure.

### Gaps
- Features of the Malvern app were taken from press and the store listing; I did not install it, so map behaviour (pins vs country list), search, languages and per-miracle sourcing are unverified. The six-languages vs English-only conflict is unresolved.
- Google Play listing was not opened (install count, last update unknown).
- No Eucharistic-miracle map was found on ArcGIS StoryMaps, Google My Maps, or from the National Eucharistic Revival or Magis Center. Those platforms are poorly indexed by search, so this is "none found", not proof that none exist.
- eucharistic-miracles.net could not be rendered, so its live status and count are unconfirmed beyond the June 2026 write-up.

## 3. Which products map related Catholic content, and which are closest in design to a searchable pin map with cited summaries?

### Takeaway
The closest design relatives are The Relic Project (pin map of relics, browse by saint/place/class, launched Nov 2025) and the MiracleHunter plus National Geographic Marian apparitions map (the only one found that encodes Church approval status in the map legend). Pilgrimage apps (Catholic Go, Rome Pilgrim, EWTN Travel, Franciscan Pilgrimage, Jubilee25) are travel guides rather than cited reference maps.

### Cited Findings
- **The Relic Project** — therelicproject.org (map at /displays). Founder Anthony Di Mauro; unveiled 8 Nov 2025. Billed as the world's first interactive digital map of relics; browse by saint, location, or classification. Team reports finding 600 relics in Spokane, Washington alone. Donation-funded; plans include a relic chapel and a "Department of Relic Authentication and Restoration". Names Carlo Acutis a patron and cites his catalogue as the model. — [NC Register](https://www.ncregister.com/news/the-relic-project-world-first-map-of-relics)
- Aleteia covered it again on 26 Feb 2026 as aiming to catalogue all relics in the world; a search summary describes the map as "currently" listing 600 relics and gives the address as TheRelicProject.com, which conflicts with the .org address in the NC Register. — [Aleteia](https://aleteia.org/2026/02/26/the-relic-project-wants-to-catalog-all-relics-in-the-world/); [NC Register](https://www.ncregister.com/news/the-relic-project-world-first-map-of-relics)
- **MiracleHunter.com** — run by Michael O'Neill. Catalogue of Marian apparitions with approval lists, visionary biographies, summaries, messages, official Church statements and photos, sorted into categories such as traditionally approved, bishop approved, Vatican recognised, no decision. The approved-apparitions page cites a May 2016 decision as the most recent bishop approval, a sign that parts are dated. (Search summary) — [MiracleHunter approved apparitions](https://miraclehunter.com/marian_apparitions/approved_apparitions/index.html)
- **National Geographic "500 years of Virgin Mary sightings" map (2015)** — built on O'Neill's data; legend distinguishes Vatican-approved apparitions from others. — [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/news/2015/11/151113-virgin-mary-sightings-map/); [Aleteia](https://aleteia.org/2019/05/15/this-map-illustrates-500-years-of-marys-apparitions)
- Big Think's description of the legend: colour-coded dots for tradition-only, unconfirmed, approved as worthy of faith, with larger dots for Vatican-recognised apparitions. — [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/strange-maps/world-map-marian-apparitions)
- The NatGeo education version shows major apparitions "with the exception of Europe". (Search summary) — [National Geographic Education](https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/visions-mary/)
- **University of Dayton Marian Library (IMRI)** — reference lists of approved apparitions and statistics; a search summary says the site is no longer being updated with new content as of 2024 and its modern directory runs through 2011. — [University of Dayton](https://udayton.edu/imri/mary/a/apparitions-approved.php)
- **Catholic Go** — catholicgo.com, founded by Christian and Darcie Nielsen, technology by Maximilian Guilbert. Interactive US map of sacred sites, historic churches, shrines, relics and pilgrimage destinations, with curated guides, community reviews, trip-planning tools, nearby family stops, and partner listings and discounts for shrines, parishes and Catholic businesses. A website as of Oct 2025, with an app in development. — [Aleteia](https://www.aleteia.org/2025/10/19/move-over-pokemon-go-its-time-for-catholic-go/)
- **Rome Pilgrim** (iOS; by Franciscan friars, covered Feb 2025) — guide to over 170 relics and tombs of saints plus churches, basilicas, art and icons in Rome, with descriptions, history and prayers per site. (Search summary) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6739810582); [Aleteia](https://aleteia.org/2025/02/11/franciscan-friars-launch-app-to-guide-pilgrims-in-rome/)
- **EWTN Travel** (iOS) — curated maps of Rome, the Holy Land and European cities. (Search summary) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ewtn-travel/id6670783456?uo=4)
- **Franciscan Pilgrimage** (iOS) — 37 Franciscan sites on an interactive map, fully offline. (Search summary) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6760918231)
- **Jubilee25** (iOS) — Jubilee 2025 pilgrimage tracker for marking visited churches. (Search summary) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/it/app/jubilee25/id6590631281?l=en-GB)
- **Catholic GeoHub (GoodLands)** — launched 2019 by Molly Burhans's nonprofit on Esri ArcGIS Hub; maps every diocese boundary worldwide plus statistical layers; free to view, with data described as viewable and downloadable. Church-administration data, not sacred sites. — [America](https://www.americamagazine.org/faith/2019/10/30/theres-map-goodlands-launches-catholic-geohub); [Aleteia](https://aleteia.org/2019/11/07/digital-maps-provide-treasure-trove-of-information-about-the-catholic-church-for-free)
- **GCatholic.org** — reference site with diocese maps by region. — [GCatholic](https://gcatholic.org/dioceses/)
- **American Saints and Causes** — has a maps and pilgrimages section for US saints and causes. (Search result only) — [americansaintsandcauses.com](https://americansaintsandcauses.com/content/maps-and-pilgrimages)

### Inferences
- Closest in design to Carlo's Compass: (1) The Relic Project, for a faceted pin map of a single devotional dataset, newly launched and explicitly Carlo-inspired; (2) NatGeo/MiracleHunter, for visual encoding of approval tiers. Neither is confirmed to show per-pin source citations on the map, and neither has a time slider.
- MiracleHunter's approval taxonomy (traditional, bishop, Vatican, no decision) is a ready precedent for a "Church recognition" field on Eucharistic miracle cards.
- Pilgrimage apps compete for attention, not for the same job: they optimise for visiting, reviews and commerce, which is the clutter Carlo's Compass wants to avoid.

### Gaps
- No dedicated interactive map of incorrupt bodies was found; only articles and an old (2012) Rome relics map mentioned on MetaFilter.
- Mass-finder maps (e.g. masstimes.org) returned nothing in my searches, so they are not catalogued here.
- The Relic Project's map was not opened; total pin count, per-pin sourcing and the correct domain (.org vs .com) are unverified.
- Catholic Go's site count, pricing and whether its app shipped in 2026 are unknown.
- Whether a map version of Carlo's Marian apparitions exhibition exists was not checked.

## 4. Are there Catholic Bible map or Bible geography products specifically?

### Takeaway
Thin field. Verbum (the Catholic edition of Logos) sells the deepest map content as paid add-ons, and one small new app, Kerygma, has a free Catholic-canon "Biblical Atlas" of 100+ places. No evidence was found of a Bible map feature in Hallow, Word on Fire, Augustine Institute, Ignatius or Ascension apps.

### Cited Findings
- **Verbum** — "Logos Bible Maps Volume 1" supplies maps to the built-in Atlas feature; Volume 2 is listed as a work in progress and not for sale. Verbum also resells general atlases, e.g. New Bible Atlas ($20.99), Tyndale Handbook of Bible Charts and Maps ($19.99), Baker Book of Bible Charts, Maps and Time Lines ($29.99). (Search summary) — [Verbum: Logos Bible Maps Vol. 1](https://verbum.com/product/182751/logos-bible-maps-volume-1); [Vol. 2](https://verbum.com/product/45672/logos-bible-maps-volume-2); [New Bible Atlas](https://verbum.com/product/30242/new-bible-atlas)
- Verbum's basic tier is free, with a Biblical Places Dataset and Biblical Places Maps listed as add-ons at $14.99 each. (Search summary) — [Verbum Basic](https://verbum.com/basic)
- **Kerygma - Bible Explained** (iOS/iPadOS; developer Ange Rodriguez) — free with Kerygma Plus subscription ($1.99 weekly, $4.99 monthly, $29.99 yearly). All 73 books of the Catholic canon, AI explanations, Catechism section, Latin prayers. "Biblical Atlas": interactive map of over 100 locations (ancient cities, apostolic routes, Christian heritage sites); tapping a marker shows history and related passages. No timeline. Six languages (English, French, Italian, Polish, Portuguese, Spanish). Version 1.0 on 2 May, now at version 12.0, updated within hours of my check; too few ratings to display. (Fetched) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6762185288)
- **Ascension: Catholic Bible app** — built on the Great Adventure Catholic Bible and the colour-coded Bible Timeline learning system; free with subscription at $8.99 per month or $59.99 per year; the results showed no maps feature. (Search summary) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1660909501); [Ascension](https://ascensionpress.com/pages/ascension-app-bible)
- Ascension sells printed Bible Timeline charts. — [Ascension charts](https://ascensionpress.com/collections/charts-tabs)
- **Ignatius Catholic Study Bible app** — lists concordance, doctrine index and parallel verses; complete version $49.99; maps not mentioned in the results. (Search summary) — [Ignatius](https://ignatius.com/catholic-study-bible-app-csbapp/)

### Inferences
- There is no free, web-based, Catholic-canon Bible map with cited pins. Verbum is powerful but paywalled and desktop-study oriented; Kerygma is a one-person AI app whose atlas is a side feature.
- Ascension owns the "Bible timeline" idea in the Catholic market but as a reading plan and colour system, not a geographic timeline. A map with an era slider would be complementary rather than duplicative.

### Gaps
- Hallow, Word on Fire and Augustine Institute (Formed) were searched and nothing about maps surfaced; I did not open their feature pages, so absence is not confirmed.
- Whether Kerygma's atlas cites sources per place is unknown. Its year of release is not shown on the listing (App ID range suggests 2026).
- Verbum's Atlas capabilities (search, time filtering, deuterocanonical places) were not examined first-hand.

## 5. Do any of them show Church approval status or cite sources per miracle? Do any have a timeline?

### Takeaway
Approval status appears as a designed feature only in the Marian apparition world (MiracleHunter and the NatGeo map). No Eucharistic-miracle product found shows a structured recognition status, a source list per miracle, or a miracle timeline; the nearest things are a link back to the official PDF (eucharistic-miracles.net) and a static chart over time (Stacker).

### Cited Findings
- MiracleHunter classifies apparitions by approval tier and publishes official Church statements; the NatGeo map's legend encodes Vatican approval. — [MiracleHunter](https://miraclehunter.com/marian_apparitions/approved_apparitions/index.html); [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/news/2015/11/151113-virgin-mary-sightings-map/)
- The Malvern app links each miracle to Old and New Testament verses and pilgrimage resources; coverage mentions no approval status or source citations. Its only timeline is "Live Like Carlo", about his life. — [NC Register](https://www.ncregister.com/features/how-st-carlo-acutis-inspired-this-eucharistic-miracles-app)
- eucharistic-miracles.net gives each miracle a link to the original PDF panel; filters cover country and category; no timeline is described. — [write-up](https://tomportfolio.netlify.app/posts/2026-05-27-eucharistic-miracles/)
- The official list gives only "City, year" per entry and no status or sources at list level. — [miracolieucaristici.org list](https://www.miracolieucaristici.org/en/liste/list.html)
- Stacker charted miracles over time (static) and described its set as every Eucharistic miracle "the Vatican has declared", a framing taken from the catalogue rather than from any per-miracle status. — [Stacker](https://stacker.com/stories/news/vatican-investigating-miracle-connecticut-church-heres-every-eucharistic-miracle)
- The Relic Project plans relic authentication as a future department; no current per-relic verification feature is described. — [NC Register](https://www.ncregister.com/news/the-relic-project-world-first-map-of-relics)
- Kerygma's atlas and the pilgrimage apps describe no timeline. — [Kerygma App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6762185288)

### Inferences
- A draggable timeline over place-based miracles (8th to 21st century) appears to be unique among the products found.
- Recognition status needs care: media commonly call the whole catalogue "Vatican approved", but recognition of Eucharistic miracles is usually local (bishop, cult, feast) and varies by case. A per-pin "Church recognition" field with its own citation would be both differentiating and more accurate than existing products.
- Certainty-of-location badges have no precedent in any product found.

### Gaps
- Whether individual official PDF panels name the approving authority or sources was not checked panel by panel.
- I found no authoritative public list giving the recognition status of each catalogued Eucharistic miracle.

## 6. Which have open data or allow reuse, and under what licence?

### Takeaway
No openly licensed dataset of Eucharistic miracles was found anywhere: not on GitHub, not on a data portal, and not from the official site. Every product found is either all-rights-reserved or silent on licence, so Carlo's Compass would be the first open, PR-editable dataset if it writes its own summaries and geocodes its own points.

### Cited Findings
- Official catalogue: free download for exhibiting, no licence text on the home, list or association pages. — [miracolieucaristici.org list](https://www.miracolieucaristici.org/en/liste/list.html); [carloacutis.com](https://carloacutis.com/en/association/mostra-miracoli-eucaristici)
- eucharistic-miracles-ui: code public but with no licence file; data withheld because the author does not own it. — [GitHub](https://github.com/thsmale/eucharistic-miracles-ui)

### Inferences
- Wikipedia text is CC BY-SA and Wikidata is CC0 as general policy, so they are the only clearly reusable starting points for coordinates and cross-references; the official catalogue should be linked to, not copied.
- An open licence plus a plain-file dataset would be a real first, and fits goal 4 (open and editable). Asking the association for a blessing, as Malvern did through Antonia Acutis, would reduce risk and add credibility.

### Gaps
- GitHub was not searched directly (only via web search), so small repos could exist unseen.
- The Trasancos Substack project and its data availability were not examined.
- The Wikipedia list's coverage and overlap with the 108 place-based entries were not compared.
- Catholic GeoHub's actual licence terms were not opened.
